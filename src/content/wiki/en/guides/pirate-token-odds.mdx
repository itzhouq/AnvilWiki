## What are the official Fortnite Pirate Token odds?

**1 in 137,500 chests.** Epic Games published the number in November 2026 after a week of community guessing, and it comes with two conditions worth remembering: the rate is identical for every player — no new-account or returning-player bonus — and Treasure Hours on Saturdays multiply it by 1.5x, to roughly 1 in 91,667. Epic also acknowledged that individual Treasure Hour events can run hotter, with one early window confirmed at 2x. Everything below is Epic's confirmed math, not a community estimate.

## The complete chest reward odds table

This is the full Treasure Hunters chest ladder, at Epic's officially published rates. It is the only place on this site that carries the complete table — other pages link here instead of repeating it.

| Reward | Base odds (per chest) | Treasure Hours boost | Boosted odds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly Gold Outfit | 1 in 1,562,500 | 7.5x | ≈ 1 in 208,333 |
| Pirate Shop Access (token) | 1 in 137,500 | 1.5x (sometimes up to 2x) | ≈ 1 in 91,667 |
| Weekly Vehicle & Decal | 1 in 7,813 | 10x | ≈ 1 in 781 |
| Weekly Pickaxe or Back Bling | 1 in 568 | — | — |
| Weekly Jam Track | 1 in 391 | — | — |
| Weekly Loading Screen | 1 in 272 | — | — |
| Any Gizmo | 1 in 127 | — | — |

Three reading notes. First, percentages: the token sits at about 0.00073% per chest, the gold outfit at 0.000064%. Second, each treasure type can only drop once per week (Gizmos are unlimited), so the ladder is designed to hand you the small tiers quickly and save the rare ones. Third, only the top three rows get a Treasure Hours multiplier — the daily tiers keep their base rates all week.

## How much does the Treasure Hours boost actually help?

The boosts are tier-specific: 1.5x for the token, 7.5x for the weekly gold outfit, and 10x for the vehicle tier, and the multiplier can vary by the theme of the individual Treasure Hour — Epic confirmed one 2x token window in the season's first week. Treasure Hours run twice every Saturday during the season: 2–4 PM ET and 9–11 PM ET. The schedule, along with Mastery Mondays and Captain's Days, lives in our [event schedule](/guides/).

Practically, a 1.5x token boost means roughly one-third more expected tokens for the same number of chests. It does not change the fundamental picture — the token remains a five-decimal-percentage event — but if you are grinding anyway, doing it inside the boosted windows is free math.

## How rare is the token in real numbers?

Working from the official 1 in 137,500 rate, the arithmetic looks like this:

| Chests opened | Approx. chance of at least one token |
| --- | --- |
| 360 (a thorough full-map clear) | ~0.26% |
| 10,000 | ~7% |
| 50,000 | ~31% |
| 137,500 | ~63% |

Even the last row is the uncomfortable one: opening 137,500 chests — about 380 flawless full-map clears — still leaves a one-in-three chance of never seeing a token. That is the honest shape of the grind, and it is why [what the token does when it hits](/items/fortnite-pirate-shop/) matters so much: one find buys 14 days of the entire shop.

<Callout type="warn" title="There is no guaranteed chest count">
Drop odds do not pity-timer. Chest 1 and chest 137,500 carry the same rate, and opening more chests only adds independent attempts — nothing accumulates toward a guarantee.
</Callout>

## Do the odds differ by platform or player?

No. Epic states the drop rate is identical for all players, and nothing in the official numbers distinguishes PC, console, or mobile. The community theory that mobile clients open chests faster — and therefore see more rolls per hour — is about play speed, not drop rate; it remains unproven as a real advantage and Epic has confirmed no platform-side difference. Any site claiming boosted odds for new accounts or paid members is contradicting the published numbers.

## Why did Epic have to publish the odds at all?

Because week one went sideways. Early community estimates pegged the token at around 0.1%, a "1 in 700,000" figure spread as if official, and boosting services started charging $200–250 for something that cannot be traded. Epic settled it via its official status channels on November 7–9, 2026, confirming the rates in the table above. The lesson generalizes: before trusting any odds number, check whether it comes from Epic — this site keeps the official table current and dates every revision.
